02 / Interaction design

Keep the conversation in motion.

Speaking, listening and thinking are states of one character. Their transitions should look connected, even when the answer arrives at a different speed from the animation.

Blend gestures

Authored body animation now works with a separate layer for all 30 finger joints. Relaxed, open, pointing and rounded poses blend with the wrist and arm motion. I refined the hand geometry without replacing the original character asset.

Coordinate speech

Audio energy and transcript timing help drive mouth targets and body movement, with readiness checks before speech starts. The result is approximate visual synchronisation.

Control the context

The assistant uses a maintained professional source, and server-side functions handle chat and transcription. It is designed to explain my work without pretending to know my private life.

Preserve text

The viewer is requested before the 3D model loads. If optional rendering, voice or microphone features fail, typed chat remains the alternative wherever the responding service is available.

03 / Boundaries

Where the experiment ends.

The actual application is optional. You can understand the design and its limits without opening it or granting microphone permission.

Reply quality

The character is a software interface. AI answers can be wrong, and the replies are not me personally speaking. The context limits the subject; it cannot guarantee an accurate answer.

Speech and assets

Support varies by browser and permissions. The real demo may process a voice question remotely. Code and integration are documented separately from adapted animation resources and identity artwork; the source-code license does not grant reuse of the character likeness.

The teaching map illustrates the reply architecture reviewed at frontend revision cc7771f. Version 3 (9497614) extends the character performance and studio without changing that reply path. Provider configuration can change; this is not a continuously updated dependency graph.
